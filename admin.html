Discover locations from properties & activities

Scans every onboarded property, the full StockNetwork resort list, and all activities for the areas, towns and suburbs already on file, and adds any that aren't yet a Town or Suburb here — auto-suggesting a zone and coordinates from the source records. Never touches or overwrites a town/suburb you've already added or edited.

Geocode locations from coordinates

Automatically places every property, StockNetwork resort-list row, and activity that has a latitude/longitude but no location tag yet, using Google's Geocoding API — no manual entry needed. Never touches anything already tagged, whether that was set by hand or by an earlier run of this.

Re-check zones on everything already tagged

Re-sends every coordinate through Google again — including records already tagged — and brings the whole hub up to date: each place gets its Country, Zone, Town and Suburb (the zone now comes from where the coordinates fall on the zone map, so Garden Route, Eastern Cape and same-named towns such as the two Middelburgs land correctly; outside South Africa the zone is the province/region), places with no town of their own are placed Nearby the closest town, and towns that share a name in different provinces get separate records. Every Google answer is remembered, so a coordinate is only ever paid for once — running this again later is free. It can overwrite a zone you corrected by hand, so use it as a cleanup, not routinely.

Fix towns with no zone

"Re-check all zones" above only revisits a coordinate that belongs to a current property, resort row, or activity — a Town added via "Discover & add new locations" with no matching property can be left with a blank zone forever, no matter how many times that runs. This instead looks at every town that already has coordinates but no zone, and works out its zone directly from those coordinates (the zone map first, a Google lookup only as a fallback). Never touches a town that already has a zone set.

Merge duplicate towns

Finds towns that are the same place recorded more than once (same name, same country, same zone, close together — for example the four “Cape Town” entries) and joins them into one. Their suburbs, photos and everything tagged to them (properties, activities, hooks) move to the town that is kept. Towns that only share a name but are in different zones (Heidelberg in the West Coast & Overberg and in Gauteng) are not touched. Find duplicates only shows you the list — nothing changes until you press Merge.

Location tree audit

"Merge duplicate towns" above only catches two Town records with the exact same name, country and zone — that's deliberately narrow so it's safe to auto-merge. This audit looks for two patterns it can't see: (1) the same real place recorded under names that differ by a word, like "Inhambane" and "Inhambane Province" — caught by treating one name as the other plus a qualifier word; (2) a place that exists as its own standalone Town and as a Suburb nested under a different Town, like "Brenton-on-Sea" and "Brenton" (a suburb of Knysna). It also checks that every property, resort row and activity is actually nested under a Town/Suburb that still exists, and flags anything tagged with an area/zone but never linked down to a Town at all. Nothing here is fixed automatically — a name match can occasionally be two genuinely different places that happen to share a word, so every result is for you to check and then fix by hand with the Edit / Delete / Merge tools above.

Improve property coordinates (from your StockNetwork export)

Most StockNetwork coordinates are rounded to about 1 km, so map pins can sit a kilometre out. Choose your StockNetwork resort export (the .csv you upload to the resort list) and this looks each property up by name on Google, then gives you two files: a review report (every property with a confidence rating and the suggested latitude/longitude), and a copy of your export with only the high-confidence coordinates swapped in, ready to load back into StockNetwork. It changes nothing in the hub itself. Each property looked up is one Google lookup.

Corrected StockNetwork file (Country / State / City / Suburb from the hub)

Makes the file you send to StockNetwork match this hub. Choose your StockNetwork resort export and you get a copy in exactly the same format where, for every property, Country, State, City and Suburb are filled in from the hub's Zone > Town > Suburb, worked out from the property's coordinates. State is the hub zone (for example Garden Route, Eastern Cape, or the province/region for places outside South Africa). When a place has no suburb, the town name is repeated; when it has no town of its own but is close to one, the Suburb reads Nearby <Town>. Every other column is left exactly as it was.

Run Re-check all zones (above) first so the hub is up to date — then this is quick and needs no new Google lookups. Rows the hub can't settle (coordinates missing or in the sea, or the country in the file disagreeing with where the coordinates are) are left as they were and listed in the report for you to decide. Nothing is sent to StockNetwork from here.

How Payouts Work

Where the numbers come from, and how to get each affiliate paid

1

Add the affiliate here first

On the Affiliates tab, add each affiliate with their exact Hub affiliate ID (the same ID that's in their personal Hub link and their flight/booking links) and set what they earn for each channel — Accommodation, Flights, Activities, Car Hire, and Packages. These match the tabs in their own Hub. For each channel choose either a % of the revenue you log, or a fixed R flat amount paid every time you log an entry for that channel — whichever suits how you pay that affiliate.

2

Pull flight booking numbers from Impact.com

Log into app.impact.com, then in the top navigation go to Reports → More Reports → Performance by Sub ID and Shared ID (or Reports → Advanced Action Listing for a booking-by-booking view). Every affiliate's flight bookings are tagged with their Hub affiliate ID as the Sub ID, since the Hub's "Your Flight Link" appends it automatically. Filter or group by Sub ID to see each affiliate's revenue for the period.

3

Pull accommodation numbers from StockNetwork

Accommodation bookings run through each affiliate's own personal StockNetwork booking link rather than through Impact.com, so check StockNetwork's own reporting for booking revenue per affiliate over the same period.

4

Log the revenue on the Payouts tab

Select the affiliate, choose which channel the revenue came from (Accommodation, Flights, Activities, Car Hire, or Packages), enter a period label (e.g. "August 2026") and the total revenue for that period, then click Add Revenue Entry. The amount you owe them is calculated automatically using that channel's own revenue share percentage.

5

Pay them, then mark it paid

Once you've paid an affiliate (EFT, etc.), find that entry in their Payouts table and click Mark Paid. This updates their outstanding balance so you always know who's still owed money at a glance.

💡 If you enter more than one revenue entry for the same period by mistake, just delete the wrong one from the entries table — deleting an entry automatically reverses its effect on that affiliate's totals.
